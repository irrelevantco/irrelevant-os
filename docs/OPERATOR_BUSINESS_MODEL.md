# iRRELEVANT OS: a guided operator for local business growth

Direction proposed November 24, 2026, following the request to use Foundable's Ted as a business-model reference. This is a product and commercial roadmap, not an announcement that future features or prices are live.

## What the reference establishes

Foundable presents Ted as an AI employee for each business: a conversational entry point, connected services, research and drafts, work history, approvals and payments into the owner's own Stripe account. Its published prices currently include Basic $25/month, Pro $50/month and Max $200/month, with weekly work allowances. Its AI disclosure identifies external providers including OpenAI and Anthropic. These are the vendor's published descriptions, not independently verified customer results.

The useful pattern is an understandable operator experience tied to a particular business and observable tasks. Implement our own experience and workflows from our own requirements. No account, purchase, data transfer, copying of proprietary outputs or dependency on Foundable is proposed.

## Our offer

Working positioning: **iRRELEVANT OS helps established local businesses organize inquiries, approve follow-ups, schedule agreed conversations and track work through to payment.**

The business owner works through iR Agent. The CRM, files and integrations support that experience. The first commercial product is one well-defined growth workflow with implementation, training and support. Choose one initial customer segment and prove the workflow before expanding into employee, payroll or production systems.

Differentiate through local implementation, source-backed research, spreadsheet reconciliation, industry-specific workflows and accountable support. A $10,000–$25,000 engagement needs a defined service scope and evidence of value; a generic assistant interface alone does not establish that price.

The agency's own prospecting workflow and a client's workflow need separate target definitions. For example, commercial cleaners may research public business prospects; a residential service company may primarily need help with inbound inquiries, consented customer follow-ups and booking. Do not present a list of local companies as a source of qualified residential customers.

## Commercial structure to validate

Retain the existing service-offer ladder while making the operator the customer-facing experience:

1. **Scoped pilot, proposed $750 one-time:** one company, one documented workflow, an agreed input sample, reconciliation, setup and training. Agree on deliverables, duration and success criteria before quoting. This is a pricing hypothesis, not a published entitlement or proven ROI.
2. **Implementation:** use the existing Digital Foundation or Growth Launch range when the agreed website, workflow and migration scope fits. Separate one-time work, software rights and continuing costs in the proposal.
3. **Managed growth partnership:** the existing $10,000/year offer can combine operator access with specifically limited monthly service and support, under the signed 12-month agreement and four $2,500 installments. Provider charges, quotas, hosting, support hours, changes and exclusions must be stated.
4. **Custom infrastructure partnership:** consider the existing $25,000/year tier after a larger organization's scope, permissions, integrations, migration and support requirements have been established and costed.

A software-only license can become a separate offering after paid pilots establish reliability, operating costs, support burden and customer demand. Do not promise unlimited hosted AI or lifetime free operation. A one-time software license does not eliminate ongoing hosting, email, model, storage or maintenance costs. Keep customer-owned provider accounts or explicit included allowances, with hard caps and no automatic paid upgrades.

## Owner experience

The proposed navigation starts with one business and its current objective. Existing guided choices remain available:

- Find fitting business prospects / import existing records.
- Research a selected company with sources and verification status.
- Prepare and review an outreach draft or follow-up.
- Review replies and propose actual available meeting times.
- Review proposals and project payments, once implemented.
- Review completed work, exceptions, usage and costs; pause work.

Each action should expose the inputs, artifact, owner approval where required and final outcome. A future natural-language layer can select validated tools; it must not bypass the existing server and database authorization. The built-in rule-guided interface is not a trained proprietary model. Optional AI providers have real usage costs.

## Honest implementation status

Currently implemented: organization-based CRM and RLS, CSV preview/import, spreadsheet conversion, editable sourced reviews, guided task choices, bounded preparation/automation controls, and Google integration code. Phone access uses the existing hosted app. Local and hosted accounts, records and OAuth configuration are separate.

Not established by those features: hosted Google setup for every account, an always-on hosted worker, software licensing/entitlements, automatic payment reconciliation, signed contracts, site generation/publishing tools, inbound voice agents, payroll execution or a general production system. These need their own implementation and acceptance checks. Existing manual approval and bounded-campaign rules remain authoritative.

## Delivery order

1. Fix and verify large CSV preview/commit reliability, full-row review and clear failure messages.
2. Complete one owner-operated cycle using real records: qualified lead, approved evidence, real conversation, agreed meeting, written scope, signed acceptance, verified payment and delivery.
3. Connect project payments in test mode, then an explicitly approved live payment flow. Record cash separately from proposals and opportunity estimates. Client receipts settle to their own merchant account when acting for a client; platform routing for others is a separate integration scope.
4. Add reliable hosted job execution with leases, retries, idempotency, budgets and stop controls. Select and approve any paid hosting only when needed; free Render sleeping and iOS suspension do not provide continuous work.
5. Build the pilot's business profile, service-area/capacity settings, workflow template and measurable work history. Reuse existing tenant isolation and export paths.
6. Deliver a small number of paid, supported pilots. Use their actual usage costs, support time, accepted deliverables and client outcomes to decide software-only packaging and prices.

No sales claims, public pricing, billing, outgoing communications, publishing permissions or production branding are changed by this roadmap.
