# Company workspace direction

The near-term product remains the acquisition workspace used by iRRELEVANT CO. Prove the complete lead → verified review → approved preparation → conversation → signed work → delivery process with real, permissioned records before selling outcomes. No client revenue, savings or conversion performance is established by the software itself.

The next commercial hypothesis is a flat-rate company installation/license plus a clearly scoped migration and support service. Companies could receive one focused template: acquisition (Leads/Pipeline/Research), workforce (Employees/Schedule/Applicants), or production (Orders/Operations/Delivery). The present converter is shared transition tooling; these alternative operational modules and configurable navigation are not implemented yet. Renaming a CRM lead to an employee is not a valid HR implementation.

## Pilot acceptance before selling an annual engagement

1. Run the acquisition pilot and measure qualified prospects, research time, actual logged conversations, signed value, collected cash and delivery costs. Keep estimates separate from verified facts.
2. Run a synthetic legacy-export conversion, then an authorized redacted customer sample. Record mappings, exceptions, source/output counts and client-approved reconciliation. Do not upload employer exports or resumes to public repositories.
3. Choose one company's workflow. Define roles and permissions, required records, retention, approvals, reporting and destination import format before promising replacement.
4. Build that template's real tables, tenant RLS and workflows. Workforce/payroll work needs separate sensitive-data access, audit, retention, legal and payroll expertise; a spreadsheet converter alone is insufficient.
5. Rehearse cutover, export/rollback and staff onboarding. Obtain written acceptance and define ownership, hosting, backups, updates and support obligations.

$750 projects and $10k–$25k/year agreements are pricing hypotheses to validate through scoped paid pilots, not proven savings or warranted ROI. A flat software license and ongoing migration/support engagement can be priced separately. Hosting, provider usage, support hours, implementation scope and cancellation/export terms must be explicit. Never imply a subscription replaces a signed annual agreement.

Keep OpenStreetMap attribution and ODbL obligations with map-derived data, including when redistributing a database. A software license does not make that source data exclusive. Do not advertise vendor integrations, payroll processing, employee scheduling or production management until implemented and tested.
